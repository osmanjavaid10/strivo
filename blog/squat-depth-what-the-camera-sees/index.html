Squat depth: what the camera actually sees

Inside Strivo’s form check: how 17 tracked points become one useful cue, and why we only ever give you one at a time.

Marcus Oyelaran · Strength Lead · October 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Strivo Journal

“Go deeper” is the most common squat cue in the world, and one of the least useful. Deeper than what? Measured how? When we built Strivo’s form check, we wanted every cue to be something a camera could actually see — and something you could act on mid-set.

Seventeen points, thirty times a second

When you prop your phone up for a squat, a compact pose model running on your phone’s neural engine finds 17 points on your body in every frame: ankles, knees, hips, shoulders, wrists, elbows and a handful on the head for orientation.

From those points we compute what a coach watches for:

  • Depth — the angle at the knee and whether the hip crease drops below the top of the knee
  • Knee track — whether the knees follow the line of the toes or cave inward
  • Tempo — how long the lowering, pause and drive phases take
  • Bar path — how far the wrists drift forward of the mid-foot

Every rep gets a form score from 0 to 100. The score isn’t a grade; it’s a trend line. Watching it climb from 78 to 94 over a month is more motivating than any badge.

Why one cue at a time

Early prototypes showed every problem at once. Testers hated it. A list of four corrections in the middle of a heavy set doesn’t make you lift better — it makes you think, and thinking is slow.

Coaching is choosing what not to say.

So Strivo ranks problems by how much they matter for safety and performance, and gives you only the top one. Fix your knee track and, next set, you might hear about tempo. It’s how good coaches have always worked.

Nothing leaves your phone

Frames are processed in memory and discarded within milliseconds. We never record, upload or store video or images. What we keep is the numbers — angles, depths, tempos — so your progress view can show your technique improving over weeks.

Where it struggles

We’d rather be honest about the limits. Very baggy clothing, poor lighting and filming from directly in front can all reduce accuracy. When the model isn’t confident, Strivo says so and stays quiet instead of guessing. A wrong cue is worse than no cue.
